ABOUT

Building instruments to observe the universe's rarest particles.

I am a neutrino physicist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and a Visiting Associate Professor at the ZERO Institute, Tohoku University.

My research focuses on the development of next-generation detector technologies for neutrino experiments, with an emphasis on liquid argon time projection chambers, cryogenic instrumentation, and detector commissioning.

Beyond research, I enjoy communicating science through public talks, media appearances, and educational outreach, with the goal of making frontier physics accessible to broader audiences.

MY JOURNEY

From Curiosity to Discovery

From Japan to the United States and Europe, my research journey has been driven by a simple question: How can we build better detectors to better understand our universe?

2016

🇯🇵 Japan

A curiosity about the universe.

My fascination with physics began in Japan, where I first became inspired by the idea of exploring the universe through science.

2018

🇺🇸 Harvard University

Learning how to ask scientific questions.

Ph.D. in Physics, working on Supernova Neutrinos, Solar Neutrinos, Q-Pix, and the Detector Wire Analyzer while developing next-generation detector concepts.

2024

🇨🇭 CERN

Building detectors capable of answering them.

Working on detector commissioning, cold electronics, and large-scale detector validation for DUNE.

2026

🇺🇸 Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

Inventing technologies for future discoveries.

Chamberlain Fellow developing detector R&D, pixelated charge readout systems, and commissioning technologies for future neutrino experiments.

2026

🇯🇵 Tohoku University

Connecting science across borders.

Visiting Associate Professor at the ZERO Institute, strengthening collaborations between Japan and the global scientific community.
